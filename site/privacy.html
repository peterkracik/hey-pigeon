Privacy Policy

Last updated: 2026-11-19

Hey Pigeon runs on your device and talks directly to your Google account. It has two optional features that use a small relay server, described below; neither ever receives your email. The developer does not see your email, contacts, or account data. For those two features the app sends the relay a Google access token for your mailbox, to show who is asking: the relay checks it with Google, never uses it to read mail, and does not store it.

What data Hey Pigeon accesses

When you connect a Google Account, Hey Pigeon requests the following scopes, used only to provide the corresponding feature:

  • gmail.modify — read your mail and change labels (archive, mark read/unread, trash).
  • gmail.send — send mail on your behalf when you compose or reply.
  • userinfo.profile — your name and avatar, shown in the app.
  • contacts.readonly / contacts.other.readonly — autocomplete when composing.
  • drive.appdata — a hidden, per-app folder in your Drive (invisible in the Drive UI, readable only by Hey Pigeon) used to sync reminders across your own devices.

Where your data lives

All Gmail data Hey Pigeon reads is stored in a local database on your device only. It is never transmitted to any server operated by the developer. The relay described below never receives mail. OAuth tokens are stored in your operating system's secure keychain, never in plain text.

The relay (optional)

Push notifications on iPhone and iPad go through a relay that sees your email address and a change counter and stores a one-way hash of the address with your device's push token. To register, the app sends the relay your Google access token, which the relay checks with Google and does not store.

Read status is off unless you turn it on for an account. When it is on, each message you send from that account contains an invisible image, and the relay stores a random identifier for that message, a one-way hash of your address, and the time of each open (or that the message reached a device that hides opens), for 30 days. It sees the IP address and mail app of whoever loads the image while answering the request. The relay's code does not store or log them, and its request logs are discarded, not stored. The app sends the relay your Google access token, checked with Google and not stored, to register a message and to ask what was opened. If push notifications are on as well, the relay sends your iPhone or iPad a silent push through Apple when one of your messages is opened, so the app asks sooner; that push says only that something was opened, not which message or by whom.

Turning read status off stops it on that device at once: nothing more is added to mail sent from it, and it asks the relay to delete the account's records, right away if it is online and otherwise when it next syncs. Your other devices follow when they next sync; until then a device that has not heard may still send a message with the image. Removing the account from a device asks the relay for the same deletion, which also clears read status for mail already sent from your other devices. If the account is removed or signed out before the relay could be reached, its records are left to expire within 30 days. What a device has already recorded about opened mail (the status in the message and the Recent Opens list, which shows the last 90 days) stays on that device until the account is removed from it; the relay's own 30-day limit does not shorten it. Once it is off on a device and that request has been answered, the device does not contact the relay about that account. Tracking opens can require the recipient's consent under EU and UK law; you are responsible for that when you turn it on.

AI features (optional)

Hey Pigeon works without AI. Its AI features (summaries, drafting and rewriting, translation, “Ask my inbox”, reading an attachment, suggestions for new mail, bundles and triage) run on a model you choose in Settings. Nothing they read or write goes to a server operated by the developer, and the developer never holds your API keys.

Who can answer:

  • Apple Intelligence on your iPhone, iPad or Mac, where the device has it. This is the default there. It needs no key and no account, and what it reads does not leave the device.
  • ChatGPT (OpenAI), Claude (Anthropic) or Gemini (Google), each with an API key you paste. Requests go from your device directly to that company's API with your key, and its own terms and privacy policy apply. Pasting a key sends one request to that company to check the key.
  • Jev (jevtypesafeai.com), a third-party classification service that is not part of Hey Pigeon, for triage only, with a key you paste. Its own terms and privacy policy apply.
  • Apple's Private Cloud Compute (Apple's servers, no key) is listed in Settings, but current builds do not include it and send nothing to it.

If the provider you prefer cannot answer on a device (Apple Intelligence is not available there, or that device has no key for it), the request goes to the next one that can. Unless you change the order, that is Apple Intelligence on the device, then ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini, whichever that device has a key for. A request can therefore go to a different provider than the one you prefer, but never to ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini without a key you pasted on that device. You can limit this fallback to the device itself, and Settings shows which provider is answering now.

What is sent when you ask for something:

  • A summary of a list: the sender, subject and preview line of the emails in it. A summary of a thread: its subject, and each message's sender name and up to 1,500 characters of its text. A summary of a bundle: the bundle's name and description, and the sender, subject and beginning of the latest message of its emails.
  • Drafting, rewriting and proofreading: the text you are working on and your instruction or notes; when drafting or rewriting, also the subject and the names and addresses of who is writing to whom; in a reply, the conversation so far.
  • Translation: the text you selected. Apple's translation on the device does it when it has the language pair; otherwise the selected text goes to the provider that answers.
  • “Ask my inbox” and reading an attachment use Apple Intelligence on the device only, whatever provider you picked. “Ask my inbox” reads the search index Hey Pigeon keeps on the device for Spotlight and Siri (subject, sender, people, labels, and message text already stored on the device); you can turn that index off in Settings.
  • Triage run from Siri or Settings uses Apple's models only. It proposes changes and applies them after you confirm; it cannot send or delete mail.

What reads mail without a tap. The first four each have a switch in Settings:

  • Auto-summarize (off unless you turn it on): summaries are made without a tap, of lists ahead of time, and of a thread or a bundle when you open it. They send what the same summary sends when you ask for it (above) to whichever provider answers, including ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini.
  • Suggest for new mail (on where the device has Apple Intelligence): the sender, subject, time and up to 4,000 characters of each new unread email are read to propose Need reply or a reminder. Apple's models only, never ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini. Nothing changes until you accept.
  • Make bundles (on where the device has Apple Intelligence): the sender, subject and preview line of each new email, with the names and descriptions of your bundles and a few subjects of emails in or taken out of them. Apple's models only.
  • Auto-triage (off unless you turn it on): the sender, subject and preview line of new mail, with the names of your labels, never the full message, go to the classifier you pick. If it cannot run, Apple Intelligence or, if you pasted its key, Jev classifies instead; ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini only when you picked them.
  • Opening a bundle summarizes it, with Auto-summarize off too, when Apple Intelligence on the device is the one answering. Nothing leaves the device for it.

API keys are stored in the system keychain of the device you paste them on. Hey Pigeon does not sync them. Your AI choices (provider, model, fallback order, the switches above, triage labels, labels you set by hand, and bundles you have edited) sync between your devices through the hidden folder in your own Google Drive. Summaries, suggestions, automatic triage results and bundles you have not edited stay on the device that made them.

Limited Use disclosure

Hey Pigeon's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs to any other app adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

No selling, no ads, no analytics

Hey Pigeon does not sell or share your data, does not show ads, does not run any analytics and does not track you. Read status, if you turn it on, tells you when mail you sent was opened (see above).

Revoking access & deleting data

You can disconnect an account at any time in Hey Pigeon's settings, or revoke access directly at myaccount.google.com/permissions. Since your mail is stored only on your device, uninstalling the app or deleting its local data folder removes it. The app asks the relay to delete its records when you turn off push or read status or remove the account, and read-status records expire on their own after 30 days.
